# Cache lifecycle transaction contract

The derived cache is optional. Canonical Project and USD publication remains authoritative. Missing, stale, partially projected, failed and retired cache entries must not be reported as fully Ready.

## Storage and synchronization

A Project cache transaction uses one stable OS lock under the qualified cache .locks directory, excluded from sweep. A thread may enter another cache owner while already holding that transaction; do not wait for another thread or invoke an external callback inside it. All cooperating processes must use this protocol. The lock is not permission to traverse a symlink, junction or other reparse point. Validate the intended derived root and each destructive path. The current safe-Rust path checks assume that external tools do not concurrently replace cache directory entries or the lock file; arbitrary non-cooperating filesystem modification is not supported. Never run destructive collection on an unqualified root. Qualification of Windows junctions and hostile concurrent pathname replacement is required before broadening that contract.

Lease JSON and a unique locked sidecar bind a reader or builder to its required resources. Version 2 records validate all lowercase 64-hex revision and blob keys and store a domain-separated BLAKE3 integrity checksum of the entire immutable reference record (excluding the checksum itself). An active legacy version 1 record without this proof defers collection; a structurally valid legacy record may be ignored only after its exact qualified sidecar is proved unlocked. Records are not rewritten or deleted during this check. A locked sidecar without its JSON record is also uncertain reachability. Empty build reservations and valid not-yet-committed revision keys remain supported. Committed digest-shaped revision entries must be ordinary directories; an unexpected type aborts collection before sweep. This checksum detects accidental corruption, not a coordinated rewrite of record and checksum. Closing a lease handle releases its lock without a panicking custom destructor. A corrupt or pre-migration pin is an uncertain reachability root and blocks collection; no PID timeout guesses are used. Immutable shared CAS is collected only under the same transaction. Legacy top-level and per-Scene payload pools and Scene indexes remain unswept even when no lease is recorded: some readers are not migrated yet. Retirement persists the generation floor before removing the mutable descriptor. No physical deletion of legacy payloads is allowed until every consumer owns a lease through its last use. Revision staging directories are the only recursively swept temporary directories. Do not remove the Project lock or its parent while any cooperating cache process may operate.

Managed cache paths are qualified component by component before creating a descendant or opening a file. The intended derived suffix is resolved from the Project layout, not from an arbitrary alias target. Existing links, junctions, reparse points, unexpected types and traversal components are refused. Standalone cache paths retain their independent directory contract. These checks do not claim protection against arbitrary non-cooperating concurrent replacement of filesystem entries.

## Desired state and recovery

Managed Scene Ready reads and publication require a present durable desired
record with the exact generation, configuration, owned-source hash and full
revision digest, including after acknowledgement. A missing record is a miss,
not authorization. State-only descriptor writes cannot create managed Ready
pointers; complete artifact publication owns that transition. Existing
Building or Ready pointers without an intent are reconciled under a fresh
authorized generation at recovery; a late worker cannot create one. Qualified
Partial metadata remains distinct from Ready and retains its exact-path repair
contract.

Queue owners alone keep the admission sender alive. The worker holds a weak
reference to it and may acquire a temporary sender only for retry/maintenance,
never while waiting for a job. Last-owner destruction releases channel
ownership and detaches the worker handle without mutex acquisition, joining or
disk I/O. Explicit shutdown closes admission and detaches without joining.
Poisoned admission is treated as unavailable, not recovered with poisoned data;
unfinished durable obligations remain recoverable on a subsequent queue.

A desired generation record is written before admission for Scene, Model and ProjectRoot targets. Acknowledgement marks the exact known identity and generation complete instead of erasing the high-water mark. Non-Scene descriptor envelopes persist the desired generation and full revision digest alongside the existing descriptor. Their readers and publishers compare source closure, recipe, schema, generation and retirement floor; old managed records without the envelope are misses. Unknown identities remain pending and cannot publish Ready. Retirement keeps a durable floor; a validated manifest member may advance beyond it, but a stale worker may not clear the floor. If recovery discovers a different canonical identity, it advances the generation instead of replaying the obsolete desired record. If the desired record is newer than its Scene pointer, recovery reconstructs the Building pointer. An unavailable graph records a Project-wide invalidation barrier. The manifest read, all durable target transitions and barrier clear are serialized by one Project transaction; a later invalidation cannot be cleared by an older reconciliation. Queue admission occurs after releasing the transaction and remains advisory. Current eligibility rejects a barrier; unresolved source identity stays ineligible even after a complete durable reconciliation.

## Identity and immutable retention

Scene-owned source hashes remain distinct from the full composed dependency and resolver context. Full identity incorporates the existing standalone dependency collector, authored context, recipe and schema. An unknown or changed identity cannot publish a fully Ready generation. Full revision identity is derived from the exact sealed `LiveStage` source buffers (including the outer USDZ archive) used by geometry, material, texture and animation extraction. Capture freezes the observed authored/anchored identifier results and physical path bindings, validates them before sealing, and captures verified Windows short-name component spellings while the filesystem is still available. Authoritative composition, visual composition and late texture reads share these bindings. Sealed lookup uses only captured keys and lexical equivalents, including mixed long/short Windows components, never a live canonical-path lookup. Unobserved unsupported aliases fail closed. The deterministic resolver identity includes the framed sorted binding tables and uses a new provenance discriminator; pre-binding persistent identities are misses. Unresolved dependency discovery or failed late texture reads makes persistent Ready ineligible; ordinary source presentation may still use fallback materials. Scene placements are read from that same stage rather than reopening the canonical path. Non-Scene extraction shares the captured stage with `revision_digest_from_snapshot`; standalone hydration and publication bind the opened stage's entire closure, not just its root bytes. The owned-target hash remains an additional live manifest/ownership fence; the composed digest is the actual payload provenance. Publication still compares live identity and durable generation under the Project transaction. Old unsealed identity schemas miss under the new recipe/schema digest; their historical bytes are not rewritten. Unsupported or incomplete snapshot capture fails closed to source fallback. Exact-path repair can restore only the already-qualified geometry digest and byte size, without replacing qualified spatial metadata. Partial metadata-only records may still be useful as non-ready metadata; they are not a complete cache hit. Read eligibility checks the full identity, index, spatial index and referenced blobs. A retained revision is created once via private staging; promotion rebases the mutable generation, never the existing immutable record. The reference set is independently derived from the verified index and material textures before accepting a retained root. Qualified structural corruption, missing required bytes and digest mismatches trigger reconstruction from canonical source. Operational I/O errors, aliases and valid-but-different records are not treated as corruption. After staging and verifying the complete replacement, an unleased damaged directory is atomically moved to a unique `.quarantine.<uuid>` sibling before create-once promotion. A worker may exclude only its own build lease from the repair check. Shared corrupt bytes can be replaced only with verified same-digest content and no other active leases. Quarantine is not a committed revision or an automatic sweep target. Interrupted staging or quarantine remains recoverable on the next qualified build. Valid historical records are never rewritten.

## Remaining integration notes

The existing bounded Project warm worker also executes coalesced per-Project maintenance jobs. Successful logical retirement requests maintenance after its Scene/Project transaction has ended; warm completion and registered-Project startup provide additional opportunities. A full or unavailable queue defers advisory maintenance to a later lifecycle/startup trigger, never rolls back canonical changes. The worker uses the existing default `GcPolicy`, holds no queue or Scene lock during collection, and logs completed counts or an abort/error. Unknown LOD catalogue reachability aborts collection before sweep; a qualified absent catalogue is normal optional absence. Current LOD catalogues reference legacy Scene pools, which are not swept. Migration of legacy hydration/residency consumers onto durable leases remains separate; keep those pools and indexes unswept until lease coverage is complete. Corrupt or unreadable desired-generation/tombstone records preserve the invalidation barrier and require qualified state reconciliation rather than silently resetting the floor. Quarantine retention and the history budget are maintenance-policy decisions; historical records remain protected while pinned or within policy. No canonical USD, Git, or manifest rewrite is performed by cache maintenance.
